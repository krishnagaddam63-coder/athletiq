🏃 Athletics Drills

Sprint faster, jump higher, throw farther. Track & field drills for young athletes aged 8–18.

A-Skips & High Knees

Sprints
Beginner

Warm-up drill focusing on knee drive, posture, and active foot strike for efficient sprinting mechanics.

⏱ 10 min 🔄 3 x 20m 👤 Solo
▶ Watch on YouTube 📺

Steps

  1. Start with a bouncing, rhythmic skip
  2. Drive your lead knee high up to hip level, keeping the toe pulled up (dorsiflexion)
  3. Strike the ground powerfully right beneath your center of mass
  4. Use aggressive arm action, driving elbows straight back

💡 Coach Tip

The goal is vertical force, not horizontal distance. Put force into the ground.

Flying 30s

Sprints
Beginner

Work on maximum velocity mechanics by building up speed before holding max pace for 30 meters.

⏱ 15 min 🔄 4 sprints 👤 Solo
▶ Watch on YouTube 📺

Steps

  1. Set up a 20m acceleration zone and a 30m max velocity zone
  2. Build up speed to 95% effort through the first 20m
  3. Hold that maximum speed flawlessly through the 30m zone
  4. Decelerate slowly over the next 20m

💡 Coach Tip

During max speed, stay relaxed inside. Tensing your face, neck, or shoulders will dramatically slow you down.

Block Clearances

Sprints
Intermediate

Practice the explosive first three steps out of the starting blocks to improve acceleration phase.

⏱ 15 min 🔄 8 starts 👤 Solo
▶ Watch on YouTube 📺

Steps

  1. Set up starting blocks and assume the "Set" position
  2. On "GO", violently throw your arms opposite to your legs
  3. Drive out at a 45-degree angle; do not stand straight up
  4. Take three massive, powerful push-steps, then reset

💡 Coach Tip

Your first step shouldn't just be quick; it must be powerful. Push against the blocks, don't just step out of them.

Hill Sprints

Sprints
Intermediate

Use a steep incline to naturally force proper acceleration posture and build immense leg drive.

⏱ 20 min 🔄 6 sprints 👤 Solo
▶ Watch on YouTube 📺

Steps

  1. Find a moderate to steep hill (about 20-30 meters long)
  2. Sprint to the top focusing strictly on high knees and arm drive
  3. Walk back down very slowly to allow full ATP recovery (2-3 mins)
  4. Repeat for 6 total high-quality reps

💡 Coach Tip

Hills penalize poor mechanics instantly. If you stand too upright, you'll lose all power.

Resisted Sled Pulls

Sprints
Intermediate

Sprint while dragging a weighted sled to overload the nervous system and increase horizontal force production.

⏱ 15 min 🔄 5 sprints 👤 Solo
▶ Watch on YouTube 📺

Steps

  1. Attach a harness and a sled with moderate weight (about 10-15% of body weight)
  2. Lean forward into the harness
  3. Drive the legs like pistons, doing 20m sprints
  4. Unclip the harness and do one "free" sprint to feel the contrast

💡 Coach Tip

If the sled alters your sprint mechanics (making you bend at the waist instead of the ankle/hip), the weight is too heavy.

Fartlek (Speed Play)

Distance
Beginner

An unstructured interval run alternating between easy jogging and fast bursts to build aerobic and anaerobic capacity.

⏱ 30 min 🔄 Continuous 👤 Solo
▶ Watch on YouTube 📺

Steps

  1. Warm up with an easy 10-minute jog
  2. Pick a landmark (tree, sign) and run fast until you reach it
  3. Jog slowly to recover until you catch your breath
  4. Pick another landmark and sprint. Repeat continuously for 15 mins
  5. Cool down with a 5-minute easy jog

💡 Coach Tip

Keep it fun and varied. The goal is to get used to shifting gears during a race without a rigid script.

Cadence Counting

Distance
Beginner

Improve your running economy by increasing your step rate, reducing overstriding and impact forces.

⏱ 20 min 🔄 3 x 2 mins 👤 Solo
▶ Watch on YouTube 📺

Steps

  1. Run at your normal, comfortable pace
  2. Count every time your right foot hits the ground for 60 seconds
  3. Double that number to get your steps-per-minute (cadence)
  4. Try to increase your cadence to around 170-180 SPM by taking shorter, quicker steps

💡 Coach Tip

Overstriding acts as a braking force and ruins your knees. Your foot should land directly beneath you.

Tempo Runs (Lactate Threshold)

Distance
Intermediate

Run at a comfortably hard pace (8/10 effort) for a sustained period to delay the onset of lactic acid.

⏱ 40 min 🔄 20 min tempo 👤 Solo
▶ Watch on YouTube 📺

Steps

  1. Jog for 10 minutes to warm up
  2. Settle into a pace that is "comfortably hard" (you could speak in short sentences, but prefer not to)
  3. Hold this exact pace evenly for 20 continuous minutes
  4. Jog for 10 minutes to cool down

💡 Coach Tip

The biggest mistake runners make is running tempo runs too fast. It's not a race; it's threshold training.

1km VO2 Max Repeats

Distance
Intermediate

Brutal long intervals designed to increase your maximum oxygen uptake and mental toughness.

⏱ 45 min 🔄 5 x 1000m 👤 Solo
▶ Watch on YouTube 📺

Steps

  1. Warm up thoroughly on the track
  2. Run 1000m (2.5 laps) at your 5K race pace (90% max heart rate)
  3. Rest for exactly 3 minutes (light walking)
  4. Repeat 5 times, aiming for consistent times on every rep

💡 Coach Tip

If your first 1km is done in 3:30, and your last is 4:00, you started too fast. Pace correctly.

Long Slow Distance (LSD)

Distance
Intermediate

Build mitochondrial density and capillary beds by performing the essential weekly long, slow run.

⏱ 60-90 min 🔄 1 run 👥 Group
▶ Watch on YouTube 📺

Steps

  1. Choose a scenic, flat route
  2. Run at a very relaxed, conversational pace (Zone 2 heart rate)
  3. Maintain this for 60 to 90 minutes without stopping
  4. Focus on relaxed breathing and posture

💡 Coach Tip

To run fast, you must run slow. This run builds the massive aerobic base that fuels your speed workouts.

Standing Broad Jumps

Jumping
Beginner

Develop fundamental explosive horizontal power required for the long jump and triple jump.

⏱ 15 min 🔄 3 x 5 jumps 👤 Solo
▶ Watch on YouTube 📺

Steps

  1. Stand with feet shoulder-width apart behind a line
  2. Drop into a quarter squat while swinging arms deeply backward
  3. Throw arms forward furiously while violently extending hips, knees, and ankles
  4. Land softly on both feet with a bend in the knee

💡 Coach Tip

The jump is generated 50% by your legs and 50% by your arm swing. Don't forget the arms.

Box Jumps

Jumping
Beginner

Improve vertical power and fast-twitch muscle fiber recruitment using a sturdy plyometric box.

⏱ 15 min 🔄 4 x 5 jumps 👤 Solo
▶ Watch on YouTube 📺

Steps

  1. Stand 1 foot away from a knee-to-waist high box
  2. Dip down and immediately explode upward onto the box
  3. Land softly in the center of the box in a partial squat
  4. Step down safely (do not jump backward off the box)

💡 Coach Tip

Aim for "quiet" landings. If your feet slap the box loudly, your mechanics are too stiff.

Penultimate Step Bounds

Jumping
Intermediate

Practice the specialized final two steps of a long/high jump approach to convert horizontal speed to vertical height.

⏱ 20 min 🔄 15 approaches 👤 Solo
▶ Watch on YouTube 📺

Steps

  1. Jog a 5-step approach toward a takeoff board or mark
  2. On the second-to-last step (penultimate), execute a longer, flatter step to lower your center of gravity naturally
  3. On the final stamp, plant the foot quickly and drive the free knee straight up to the sky
  4. Pop up into the air and land softly

💡 Coach Tip

Do not sink down actively; the elongated penultimate step should naturally lower your hips.

Continuous Hurdle Hops

Jumping
Intermediate

Develop reactive plyometric ability (stiffness) by bouncing swiftly over a series of hurdles.

⏱ 15 min 🔄 5 lines 👤 Solo
▶ Watch on YouTube 📺

Steps

  1. Set up 4 to 6 low hurdles spaced equal distances apart
  2. Hop over the first hurdle with two feet
  3. Minimize your ground contact time; bounce immediately over the next hurdle
  4. Keep your ankles stiff and bouncing like a pogo stick

💡 Coach Tip

Imagine the ground is lava. Spend as little time on it as humanly possible.

Single-Leg Bounding

Jumping
Intermediate

Crucial drill for triple jumpers to build unilateral leg strength and phase transitioning.

⏱ 15 min 🔄 4 x 20m 👤 Solo
▶ Watch on YouTube 📺

Steps

  1. Start with a walking approach
  2. Take off on your right foot and land on your right foot (Hop)
  3. Drive the left knee up forcefully
  4. Take off on your left foot and land on your left foot
  5. Link them continuously down the track (R-R-L-L)

💡 Coach Tip

Maintain an upright posture. If you crash heavily on landing, your core is not engaged enough.

Medicine Ball Chest Passes

Throwing
Beginner

Develop the fundamental fast-twitch pushing power required for shot put.

⏱ 10 min 🔄 3 x 10 throws 👤 Partner
▶ Watch on YouTube 📺

Steps

  1. Face your partner standing 5 meters away
  2. Hold a medicine ball at chest-level with elbows out
  3. Step into the throw and aggressively push the ball forward
  4. Flick your wrists at the end of the push for maximum velocity

💡 Coach Tip

Drive the force from your hips and legs up through your shoulders, finishing squarely.

Standing Javelin Throws

Throwing
Beginner

Isolate the upper body mechanics and block-leg mechanics of throwing without the complex run-up.

⏱ 20 min 🔄 15 throws 👤 Solo
▶ Watch on YouTube 📺

Steps

  1. Stand sideways in a split stance with throwing arm straight back
  2. Keep the point of the javelin/ball near your eye line
  3. Drive the back hip forward aggressively
  4. Block (stop abruptly) the front leg straight, acting as a catapult lever
  5. Pull the arm over the top and release

💡 Coach Tip

The front leg must remain perfectly stiff on the throw. If the knee bends, the catapult leaks power.

Shot Put Glide Drills

Throwing
Intermediate

Master the linear glide to generate immense momentum across the circle before releasing the shot.

⏱ 20 min 🔄 20 glides 👤 Solo
▶ Watch on YouTube 📺

Steps

  1. Start crouched at the back of the throwing circle facing backward
  2. Extend your free leg backward toward the throwing sector
  3. Push off your crouched leg, gliding low across the circle
  4. Land in the power position (split stance) facing sideways
  5. Immediately pivot the hips and execute the throw

💡 Coach Tip

Stay low during the glide. Don't pop up until the final throwing motion.

Discus Half-Turn

Throwing
Intermediate

Learn the rotational mechanics of the discus throw starting from the center of the ring.

⏱ 20 min 🔄 20 turns 👤 Solo
▶ Watch on YouTube 📺

Steps

  1. Stand in the middle of the circle facing sideways
  2. Swing the discus back to create tension/wrap
  3. Pivot on the ball of the right foot rapidly
  4. Whip the hips around to face the throwing area
  5. Release the discus flat with the index finger applying spin

💡 Coach Tip

The discus should be the absolute last thing to turn. Hips first, chest second, arm and implement last.

Javelin Crossover Steps

Throwing
Intermediate

Combine sprinting speed with throwing position by practicing the specific "karaoke" style approach steps.

⏱ 20 min 🔄 15 approaches 👤 Solo
▶ Watch on YouTube 📺

Steps

  1. Sprint forward holding the implement at eye level
  2. Drop the javelin back while turning sideways
  3. Perform 3 explosive crossover steps (left over right, or vice versa)
  4. On the final step, plant the block leg with immense force
  5. Execute the throw seamlessly using the momentum

💡 Coach Tip

Keep your shoulders facing the side, but your hips facing slightly forward during the crossovers.

Approach Run High Jump

Jumping
Intermediate

Learn the curved approach run and take-off technique for high jump using the Fosbury Flop method.

⏱ 25 min 🔄 8 jumps 👥 Coach needed
▶ Watch on YouTube

Steps

  1. Mark a 5-stride curved approach (J-shape) with cones
  2. Walk through the curve to feel the lean into the bar
  3. Jog the approach and practise the penultimate step (second-to-last)
  4. Practice take-off without the bar — drive the inside knee up
  5. Full jump over a low bar, landing on the mat on your back

💡 Coach Tip

Speed on the curve converts to height at take-off. Run fast, plant hard, drive the knee — and trust the mat!

Medicine Ball Throws

Throwing
Beginner

Build throwing power and rotational strength with medicine ball exercises. Foundation for shot put and discus.

⏱ 15 min 🔄 4 sets of 8 👥 Partner
▶ Watch on YouTube

Steps

  1. Set 1: Overhead throws — stand, extend ball overhead, throw forward
  2. Set 2: Chest passes — push the ball explosively from chest height
  3. Set 3: Rotational throws — twist and release sideways (both sides)
  4. Set 4: Backward overhead throws — face away, throw over your head
  5. Use a 2–3 kg medicine ball appropriate for your age and strength

💡 Coach Tip

Power comes from the ground up: push through your legs, rotate your hips, then release with your arms. The throw starts in your feet.
